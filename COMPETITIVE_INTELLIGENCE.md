## What Made Winners Win

### PlanGrid ($875M exit) — The Gold Standard for PlanO
- **Mobile-first** when competitors were desktop-only
- **Field workers as users** (not just office managers) drove viral adoption
- Grew to 12K customers and 120K paid users via bottom-up adoption
- YC pedigree + Sequoia backing opened doors
- **50% YoY growth** at time of acquisition
- Tracy Young (CEO) laser-focused on ONE use case: blueprints on iPad

### Procore ($8.3B IPO) — The Long Game
- Founded 2002, took 19 years to IPO
- Massive platform play: preconstruction, project management, financials
- 84% gross margins, strong NRR (net revenue retention)
- Vertical SaaS premium: investors love construction-specific tools
- Revenue: $0 to $1.3B over 20 years, but hockey stick after $100M ARR

### CubiCasa ($12.7M revenue, acquired) — Most Similar to PlanO
- AI-powered floor plan scanning from smartphone
- Only needed $4.6M in funding to reach $12.7M revenue
- Capital-efficient: 115 employees, ~$110K revenue/employee
- Acquired by real estate data company (Clear Capital) for data play
- **Key insight: the floor plan IS the data product, not just a drawing**

### Floorplanner (30M users, profitable) — The Bootstrap Model
- Self-funded, profitable for 10+ consecutive years
- 30 employees serving 30M users = extreme efficiency
- Freemium model with B2B enterprise tier
- Acquired their funded competitor using cash flow profits

## Common Patterns Across All 14 Companies

### 1. B2B beats B2C (always)
- PlanGrid, Procore, Buildertrend: all B2B, all the biggest exits
- Planner5D, MagicPlan: massive consumer downloads, tiny revenue
- **Rule: Consumer floor plan tools cap at ~$5-10M revenue. B2B construction tools scale to $100M+.**

### 2. Vertical SaaS commands premium multiples
- Construction SaaS trades at 6-9x ARR (vs. 4x horizontal SaaS)
- Investors pay premium for: high switching costs, low churn, regulatory moats

### 3. Capital efficiency matters more than funding
- CubiCasa: $4.6M raised, $12.7M revenue
- Floorplanner: $0 raised, profitable for 10 years
- Houzz: $664M raised, value destroyed
- **The best outcomes are capital-efficient companies acquired strategically**

### 4. The floor plan is a data asset
- CubiCasa acquired for its floor plan DATA (not the app)
- Matterport acquired for its spatial DATA (not the camera)
- **Floor plans feed into real estate valuation, insurance, renovation estimates**

### 5. Mobile/field-first wins
- PlanGrid won by putting blueprints on iPad
- CubiCasa won by making floor plans via smartphone scan
- Desktop-only tools (RoomSketcher, old Floorplanner) grow slowly

## Red Flags That Kill Companies in This Space

1. **Freemium death spiral** — millions of users, no one converts (Planner5D)
2. **Hardware dependency** — requiring special cameras/devices limits TAM (Matterport pre-phone)
3. **Consumer-only positioning** — B2C renovation is low-frequency, high churn
4. **Over-raising capital** — Houzz raised $664M and destroyed value; Floorplanner raised $0 and is profitable
5. **No data moat** — if the tool is just a drawing app, switching costs are zero
6. **Geographic concentration** — US-only limits acquirer pool; EU presence adds strategic value
7. **Ignoring mobile** — field workers need mobile; desktop-only tools die slowly

## PlanO's Unfair Advantages (vs. all 14 competitors)

1. **Near-zero cost structure** ($1/mo fixed) — no competitor operates this cheaply
2. **GPU-accelerated AI** on owned hardware — no cloud inference costs scaling with users
3. **AI floor plan detection** built-in from day 1 (competitors bolted it on later)
4. **Bitcoin payments** — unique in this space, attracts crypto-native contractors
5. **EU-first** — most competitors are US-focused; EU renovation market is $400B+
6. **15 AI business agents** running autonomously — no competitor has this operational leverage
7. **Data flywheel** — every floor plan processed improves the AI, creating a moat that widens over time
